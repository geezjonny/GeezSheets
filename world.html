Rolled World

Drop something into the world you are the world

Select a town or a wild area on the map to aim. Picking a town aims wild events at the wilds beside it.

World packs

The world is built from packs of definitions, read top to bottom. A later pack can add new things, change an existing one by repeating its name (only the lines you write change), or take it out with remove: true. Tick the packs you want, then roll a new world. Mods are off until you tick them.

    How the world runs
    1. People act on needs. Every hour each person scores sleep, food, work, company, prayer or a secret, and does the best. Gatherers go where they, or their town, believe the wild food, timber or ore is. If nobody knows, they go and look.
    2. Knowledge lives in heads. Whatever people see in the wilds or hear on the road, they carry home. At dawn they report to the council. Greedy people keep good spots to themselves, cowards exaggerate danger, the brave play it down, the lazy skip council. Gossip spreads it in taverns and on the road, getting fuzzier each time.
    3. The council only knows its ledger. It turns the town's needs (food, safety) into options built from what it knows: forage where food was seen, put more people on a known source, buy from a town it has heard has food, scout the unknown, ration, move closer to food, post bounties, raise a militia, build walls, hunt a known threat, ask a neighbour for help, keep away from a dangerous place.
    4. Towns remember what worked. Every decision is judged afterwards. What works gets tried again; what fails gets dropped. Two towns facing the same problem can learn different answers. When a scout's story turns out to be wrong, the council trusts them less.
    5. The wilds are a food web. Grass and plants feed deer, boar and fish; those feed wolves, spiders, goblins, dragons and townsfolk alike. Each dawn everything in an area shares what is there, and the strong take a bigger share. Fed and safe animals breed; hungry, hunted or crowded ones move on and settle elsewhere; starving ones die back. Hungry predators fight over food, and the loser is driven out.
    6. The wilds learn too. Packs remember where they ate. When hungry they go back there or explore. If raiding a town or a road fed them, they come back for more. Raiders, great beasts and the dead can overrun a town; its survivors flee, and settlers may later refound it.
    7. Lives leave marks. People marry, have children and grow old. Losing someone, a burned home or a bad fight can leave a scar (grief, vengeance, nothing to lose) that changes what they do and what they want. Children inherit their parents' grudges.
    8. Some lives become stories. A survivor with nothing left may turn outlaw and end up a bandit king; a grieving widow may dig at the graves until the dead walk. Each path is a list of steps in the stories pack, and can end several ways: slain, pardoned, betrayed, redeemed, avenged.
    9. Your players are part of it. Record what the heroes did in a town and the people there remember it: gratitude, grief, grudges. Add a person shaped by them, and watch what they come to want.
    10. Each town prints a paper every two days, made only from what has reached it: its own news, word from travellers, rumours and notices.
    11. Everything is a definition, in packs. Open "World packs" to add a creature, crop, job, terrain, scar, story path or deed, or switch on a mod like Coast, Grimdark or Gentle.
